# Leave and Time Off Policy

## Annual leave entitlement
Full-time employees receive 24 days of paid annual leave per year, plus the public holidays of their work location. Part-time employees receive the same entitlement pro rata. The leave year runs from 1 January to 31 December, and new joiners receive a share of the entitlement based on their start date.

## Requesting leave
Leave requests are made in the HR portal and must be approved by the line manager, who responds within three working days. Requests for more than ten consecutive days need at least six weeks' notice. During the December closing period no more than two members of a team may be on leave at the same time.

## Carrying leave over
Up to five unused days can be carried into the next year and must be taken by 31 March, after which they are lost. Carry-over is not paid out. Managers are asked to remind their teams in November so that leave is spread through the year and does not pile up at the end.

## Sick leave
Employees who are unwell must contact their manager before the start of their working day. A medical certificate is needed from the fourth consecutive day of absence. Sick pay is paid in full for the first 26 weeks in any rolling twelve-month period and at half pay for the following 26 weeks.

## Parental leave
The primary carer of a new child receives 26 weeks of leave at full pay, and the second parent receives 8 weeks at full pay. Leave can be taken in one block or in two blocks within the first twelve months. Employees give their manager at least fifteen weeks' notice before the expected due date.

## Adoption and fostering
Employees who adopt a child receive the same leave and pay as parents of a newborn, starting from the day of placement. Time off for adoption meetings and introductions is paid and does not count as annual leave. Foster carers are entitled to five days of paid leave per year for training and reviews.

## Bereavement leave
Employees receive five days of paid leave on the death of a spouse, partner, child or parent, and three days on the death of a sibling, grandparent or parent-in-law. Further time may be agreed with the line manager as unpaid leave or as annual leave. Employees do not need to provide documentary evidence in the first instance.

## Study leave
Employees working towards a recognised professional qualification may take up to ten paid days of study leave per year for exams and revision. The qualification must be approved in advance by the department head. If an employee leaves within twelve months of finishing a funded course, part of the course fee must be repaid.

## Jury service and public duties
Employees called for jury service receive their normal pay for up to ten days, after which they may claim loss of earnings from the court. Employees must show the summons to their manager as soon as it arrives. Time off for other public duties, such as serving as a magistrate, is unpaid unless agreed otherwise.

## Volunteering
Every employee may spend two paid working days per year on volunteering with a registered charity. The activity is agreed with the manager at least two weeks ahead and reported to the community team afterwards. Teams that volunteer together are asked to arrange the day so that customer service is not affected.

## Unpaid leave and sabbaticals
After three years of service an employee may request up to three months of unpaid leave, which the department head can approve if the work can be covered. Employees with seven years of service may apply for a sabbatical of up to six months, with their position guaranteed. Benefits continue during approved unpaid leave of less than one month.

## Public holidays and shift workers
Employees who work on a public holiday receive double pay or an extra day in lieu, chosen by the employee. Shift workers are rostered so that they receive at least eleven hours of rest between shifts. Rosters are published four weeks ahead, and swaps between colleagues are allowed if the team lead agrees.
