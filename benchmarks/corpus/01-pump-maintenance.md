# Pump Maintenance Manual

## Purpose and scope
This manual covers the preventive maintenance of the centrifugal pumps installed at the Riverside treatment plant. It applies to the twelve duty pumps and the four standby pumps listed in Appendix A. Contractors working on site must follow the same procedures and sign the maintenance log after every visit.

## Inspection schedule
Every duty pump must be inspected every 90 days by a certified mechanical engineer. Standby pumps are inspected every 180 days and started for ten minutes after each inspection. The inspection date is recorded in the maintenance log, and the next due date is written on the tag fixed to the pump casing.

## Lubrication
Bearings are lubricated with lithium-complex grease grade NLGI 2. Each bearing receives 25 grams of grease every 500 operating hours, never more, because over-greasing raises bearing temperature. Used grease is collected in the labelled drum beside the workshop door and removed by the waste contractor once a quarter.

## Vibration limits
Vibration is measured at the bearing housing with the handheld analyser. A reading below 4.5 millimetres per second is normal. Between 4.5 and 7.1 the pump is placed on a weekly watch list, and above 7.1 it must be taken out of service within 24 hours and reported to the maintenance supervisor.

## Seal replacement
Mechanical seals are replaced when leakage exceeds ten drops per minute or after 8,000 operating hours, whichever comes first. The replacement kit number is printed on the pump data plate. After fitting a new seal the pump runs unloaded for thirty minutes while a technician watches for leaks.

## Operating temperature
The motor winding temperature must not exceed 105 degrees Celsius, and the bearing temperature must not exceed 80 degrees. An infrared thermometer reading is taken at each inspection. If either limit is passed the operator stops the pump, lets it cool for at least one hour and calls the supervisor before restarting.

## Lockout and tagging
Before any work on a pump the electrical supply is isolated and locked with the engineer's personal padlock and a red danger tag. Only the person who fitted a lock may remove it. If that person has left site, the shift manager may authorise removal after confirming that the pump is safe and the engineer cannot be reached.

## Spare parts
The store keeps two spare mechanical seals, four bearing sets and one complete impeller for every pump model. Stock is counted on the first Monday of each month. When a spare is used the engineer enters the part number on the requisition form, and the purchasing office reorders within three working days.

## Record keeping
Every inspection, repair and test run is entered in the maintenance log, which is kept for ten years. Entries must show the pump number, the date, the engineer's name, the readings taken and any parts replaced. The plant manager reviews the log on the last Friday of every month.

## Emergency shutdown
In an emergency any operator may press the red stop button at the pump control panel, which cuts power to all pumps in the hall. After an emergency stop the pumps may be restarted only by the shift manager, who first walks the hall to check for leaks, smoke or damaged cables.

## Training
New maintenance staff complete a two-day pump safety course before working unsupervised. Refresher training is repeated every two years and takes one day. Attendance is recorded by the training coordinator, and an engineer whose certificate has expired may not sign inspection records.

## Review of this manual
This manual is reviewed once a year in March by the maintenance supervisor and the plant manager. Changes are approved by the plant manager and announced at the monthly safety meeting. The current version number and date are printed in the footer of every page, and old copies are withdrawn from the workshop.
