# Fire Safety Procedure

## Responsibility
The site director holds overall responsibility for fire safety. Day-to-day duties are delegated to the fire safety officer, currently the head of facilities. Every manager must make sure that their staff know the evacuation routes of the area where they work and attend the yearly fire briefing.

## Fire alarm testing
The fire alarm system is tested every Thursday at 10:00 in the morning, rotating through one zone each week so that every call point is tested every six weeks. The test lasts no longer than fifteen seconds. Visitors are told about the weekly test when they sign in at reception.

## Fire wardens
There is one fire warden for every twenty employees on each floor. Wardens wear a yellow vest during drills, sweep their area to check that nobody is left behind, and report to the assembly point marshal. Wardens are appointed for two years and receive a half-day training course.

## Evacuation drills
A full evacuation drill takes place twice a year, once in spring and once in autumn, and one of the two is unannounced. The target is to clear the building in under four minutes. The fire safety officer records the time, notes any problems and presents the results to the site director within one week.

## Assembly points
The main assembly point is the car park behind building B, and the secondary point is the grass area beside the north gate. Staff must stay at the assembly point until the senior fire warden says it is safe to leave. Nobody may return to a building to collect belongings.

## Fire extinguishers
Water extinguishers are placed near paper stores and offices, carbon dioxide extinguishers near electrical rooms, and foam extinguishers in the workshop. Each extinguisher is inspected monthly by facilities staff and serviced yearly by an approved contractor. Only trained staff may use an extinguisher, and only if the fire is small and the exit is behind them.

## Escape routes
Corridors, stairways and fire doors must be kept clear at all times. Fire doors may not be wedged open, and storing boxes or furniture in an escape route is a disciplinary matter. Emergency lighting along the routes is tested every month and must run for at least ninety minutes on battery.

## Hot work
Welding, cutting and grinding need a hot work permit signed by the fire safety officer. The permit is valid for one shift only. A person with an extinguisher stays at the location during the work and for sixty minutes afterwards to watch for smouldering material.

## Storage of flammable liquids
Flammable liquids are kept in the red cabinet in the chemical store, in quantities of no more than fifty litres in total. Containers are labelled and stored upright with lids tightened. Smoking and naked flames are banned within ten metres of the store, and a spill kit hangs beside the door.

## Smoking
Smoking is allowed only in the shelter by the north gate, which is at least fifteen metres from any building. Cigarette ends must be put in the metal bins provided. Electronic cigarettes are treated the same as cigarettes and may not be used inside any building.

## People who need assistance
Employees and visitors who cannot use the stairs unaided have a personal evacuation plan agreed with the fire safety officer. Two buddies are named for each person, and refuge areas with an intercom are located on each landing. The fire brigade is told about anyone waiting in a refuge area.

## Reporting a fire
Anyone who discovers a fire sounds the nearest alarm and then calls the emergency number from a safe place. The security desk meets the fire brigade at the main gate and gives them the site plan. After any fire, however small, the fire safety officer completes an incident report within 24 hours.
