# Information Security Standard

## Objectives
This standard protects the confidentiality, integrity and availability of company information. It applies to all employees, contractors and systems. The chief information security officer owns the standard, and exceptions are granted only in writing by that office for a maximum of six months.

## Passwords
Passwords must be at least 14 characters long and are rotated every 180 days. They must not contain the user's name or a company product name, and the last ten passwords cannot be reused. After five failed sign-in attempts an account is locked for fifteen minutes.

## Multi-factor authentication
Multi-factor authentication is required for email, the VPN, the code repository and every administrative console. The preferred second factor is a hardware security key, and authenticator app codes are accepted as a fallback. Text message codes are not allowed for administrator accounts.

## Laptops and mobile devices
Company laptops are encrypted with full-disk encryption and lock automatically after five minutes of inactivity. Lost or stolen devices must be reported to the service desk within two hours so that they can be wiped remotely. Personal phones may read email only after enrolling in the mobile device manager.

## Software updates
Critical security patches are installed within 7 days of release, and all other patches within 30 days. Laptops restart overnight when an update needs it. Servers are patched in a monthly window on the second Tuesday, announced a week ahead, and rolled back if the smoke test fails.

## Email and phishing
Employees must report suspicious email with the report button in the mail client. The security team reviews every report and replies within four hours on working days. Phishing simulations run every quarter, and anyone who clicks a simulated link twice in a year is enrolled in a refresher course.

## Data classification
Information is classified as public, internal, confidential or restricted. Customer contracts and payroll data are confidential, and encryption keys and unreleased financial results are restricted. Restricted data may be stored only on approved systems, and may be shared only with named recipients.

## Backups
Production databases are backed up every night and kept for 35 days, with a monthly copy kept for seven years. Backups are encrypted and stored in a different region from the primary data. A restore test is carried out every quarter, and its outcome is reported to the chief information security officer.

## Access control
Access is granted on the principle of least privilege and reviewed by the line manager every six months. When an employee leaves, accounts are disabled within one hour of their final working time. Administrators use a separate account for privileged work and never browse the internet with it.

## Security incidents
Any suspected incident is reported to the security hotline at once. The incident manager classifies it within one hour, and confirmed data breaches are reported to the regulator within 72 hours. After each major incident a blameless review is held within ten working days and its actions are tracked until they are closed.

## Third-party suppliers
Suppliers that handle confidential data must pass a security assessment before signing a contract and again every year. Contracts require breach notification within 48 hours. Procurement keeps a register of suppliers with their risk rating, which the security team reviews each quarter.

## Training and awareness
Every employee completes the security awareness course within 30 days of joining and then once a year. The course takes about 45 minutes and ends with a short quiz that requires 80 percent to pass. Managers receive a monthly report listing team members whose training is overdue.
